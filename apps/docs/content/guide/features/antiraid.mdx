Anti-Raid gives FluxCore three automatic defences — a join-rate detector, a
minimum account-age filter, and an anti-nuke watch on mass channel and role
deletions — each with an action it takes on its own, an optional
server-wide lockdown, and a history of everything it caught.

## Prerequisites

- **The bot is in your server.** The dashboard refuses every Security
  request for a server the bot has not joined.
- **You can open the dashboard for the server.** Access goes to the server
  owner and to members whose Discord permissions include **Administrator**
  or **Manage Server**. Anyone else is turned away with a permission error.
- **Everything starts switched off.** A server that has never opened the
  Security page has anti-raid off, anti-nuke off, auto-lockdown off, no
  whitelisted roles, and no log channel, with Join Threshold at 10, Time
  Window at 10 seconds, both actions set to Kick, and Deletion Threshold at
  3. Nothing is detected and nothing is actioned until you turn the
  **Anti-Raid Protection** switch on and save.
- **The bot has to outrank the people it acts on.** A kick, ban, or timeout
  is attempted only when the bot has the authority to carry it out on that
  member; when it does not, the member stays and the raid event is still
  written to the history. Quarantining strips only the roles the bot is able
  to edit, so a role sitting above the bot's own highest role survives the
  quarantine. A lockdown quietly skips any channel whose permissions the bot
  cannot change, and an automatic lockdown never tells you how many it
  locked — its Events tab row carries no channel count.
- **Anti-nuke needs the bot to be able to read the server audit log.** That
  is how it learns who deleted a channel or a role. If the audit log cannot
  be read, anti-nuke detects nothing for that deletion — no quarantine, no
  *Nuke Attempt* entry, and no automatic lockdown — while the deletion itself
  is still recorded by the channel and role deletion logs whenever you have
  those log categories switched on.
- **If the permission system is switched on** for your server — on the
  **Permissions** page in the sidebar — then the Security page needs
  **View Config** to read the settings, **Manage Config** to save them, and
  **View Events** to load the Events tab. The **Security** entry in the guild
  sidebar is shown only to admins holding View Config. An admin without View
  Config who reaches the page anyway, from a bookmark or a shared link, is
  not refused: the page renders with every control showing its built-in
  default — the switches off, Join Threshold 10, Time Window 10, both actions
  Kick, Minimum Account Age 0, Deletion Threshold 3, no whitelisted roles, no
  log channel — whatever your server is really set to. Read that as "not
  shown to me," never as "this is how the server is configured," and press
  Save Changes on it only if you mean to overwrite your real settings with
  those values. Saving without Manage Config fails with an *Insufficient
  permissions* message. While the permission system is inactive, everyone who
  can open the page at all — the server owner, and every admin holding
  **Administrator** or **Manage Server** — has full access to all five tabs.
- **A saved change reaches the bot only while the bot has no copy of your
  settings in memory.** The bot keeps its own copy of these settings in
  memory, fills that copy the first time it reads a stored configuration for
  your server, and never refreshes that copy afterwards. It reads only when
  one of the three things it watches happens — a member joins, a channel is
  deleted, or a role is deleted — so the bot's memory of your server is empty
  until the first of those occurs, and empty again after every bot restart
  until the next one occurs. An edit saved while that memory is empty is
  picked up on the next join or deletion, with no restart needed. Once the
  bot has read your settings, every later edit is stored and shown correctly
  in the dashboard but ignored by the bot until the bot restarts.

## Turning it on

Open **Security** from the guild sidebar. The page is headed *Security* and
opens with a card reading *Anti-Raid Protection*, whose switch is the master
control: with it off, none of the three detectors run, whatever the tabs
below say. Under that card are five tabs — **Join Rate**, **Account Age**,
**Anti-Nuke**, **Lockdown**, and **Events** — and at the bottom of the page
a single **Save Changes** button.

That one button saves every tab at once. Nothing you change on any tab is
stored until you press it, and leaving the page first discards the lot. A
successful save shows *Anti-raid configuration saved*.

A value outside the range listed for its box rejects the whole save —
including the changes you made on other tabs — and shows an error message
instead. Three of the boxes also treat an empty value, or a `0`, as a
request for the default: Join Threshold and Time Window each save as 10, and
Deletion Threshold saves as 3.

## What each detector does

### Join rate

Most joins are counted, but not all of them. Three kinds of join never reach
the count at all: a bot account joining the server, a member who already holds
one of the **Whitelisted Role IDs** at the instant they join, and a member the
account-age filter acted on — that filter runs first and stops there. Every
other join is counted. When the number of counted joins inside the last
**Time Window (seconds)** reaches **Join Threshold**, the member who tipped
it over gets the **Action on Detection** — and so does every further member
who joins while the count stays at or above the threshold, one at a time, so
a raid keeps being actioned until the joins slow down.

Each actioned member produces a *Join Spike* entry on the Events tab.
Actioned members are also skipped by the rest of the join handling: no
welcome message is sent for them, no auto-role is applied, and their join is
not written to the member join log.

### Account age

When **Minimum Account Age (days)** is above 0, a member whose Discord
account was created fewer days ago than that receives the account-age
**Action** the moment they join, before the join-rate count ever sees them,
and produces an *Account Age* entry on the Events tab recording their
account's age in days. At 0 the filter is off and account age is not checked
at all.

### Anti-nuke

While **Anti-Nuke Protection** is on, the bot reads the audit log after every
channel deletion and every role deletion to find who did it. Deletions by
bots are ignored. Channel and role deletions count towards the same tally per
person: when one person reaches **Deletion Threshold** deletions within 10
seconds, they are quarantined — every role the bot can edit is removed from
them — and a *Nuke Attempt* entry is written to the Events tab. Quarantining
does not kick or ban them, and the deleted channels and roles are not
restored.

### The three actions

**Action on Detection** and the account-age **Action** each offer the same
three choices:

| Choice | What happens to the member |
| --- | --- |
| **Kick** | Removed from the server. |
| **Ban** | Banned. None of their messages are deleted by the ban. |
| **Timeout** | Timed out for 10 minutes. The length is fixed and there is no control for it. |

The reason recorded in Discord's own audit log is prefixed `[Anti-Raid]` for
kicks, bans, and timeouts on joining members, and `[Anti-Nuke]` for a
quarantine.

### The master card

| Control | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Anti-Raid Protection** switch | *Enable automatic detection and response to coordinated raids, suspicious accounts, and destructive actions.* Off stops all three detectors, including anti-nuke, no matter how the tabs are set. Saved settings and recorded events are untouched. |

### Join Rate tab

*Detect mass joins by monitoring the rate of new members joining.*

| Control | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Join Threshold** | 2 to 100. How many joins inside the time window trigger detection. Lowering it makes the detector fire on smaller bursts, including ordinary ones. |
| **Time Window (seconds)** | 1 to 120. How far back joins are counted. Raising it makes a slower trickle of joins add up to a raid. |
| **Action on Detection** | Kick, Ban, or Timeout — what happens to each member caught by a join spike. |

### Account Age tab

*Automatically action accounts that are younger than a minimum age. Set to 0
to disable.*

| Control | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Minimum Account Age (days)** | 0 to 365. Accounts younger than this are actioned on join. 0 turns the filter off. |
| **Action** | Kick, Ban, or Timeout — what happens to a member whose account is too new. This is a separate choice from the join-rate action. |

### Anti-Nuke tab

| Control | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Anti-Nuke Protection** switch | *Detect mass channel/role deletions and quarantine the responsible user by removing all their roles.* Off leaves channel and role deletions unwatched even while the master switch is on. |
| **Deletion Threshold** | 1 to 20. How many channel or role deletions by the same person within 10 seconds trigger a quarantine. Setting it to 1 quarantines someone on their first deletion. |

### Lockdown tab

| Control | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Auto-Lockdown on Raid** switch | *Automatically lock all channels when a raid is detected.* On, a join spike or a quarantined nuke attempt also denies Send Messages to `@everyone` across the server's message-carrying channels, skipping any whose permissions the bot cannot change. Nothing lifts it automatically — see below. |
| **Whitelisted Role IDs (comma-separated)** | Despite the label, this is a picker over your server's roles, not a text field. A member who already holds one of these roles at the instant they join is exempt from both the account-age filter and the join-rate count. It does not exempt anyone from anti-nuke: a whitelisted role does not stop a quarantine. |
| **Log Channel ID** | A picker over your server's text channels, with a *None* option. The choice is stored, and nothing is sent to the channel — no anti-raid message is posted anywhere. The Events tab is where raid activity is readable. |

Lifting a lockdown is a Discord command, `/lockdown lift`, which requires
**Manage Server** (`ManageGuild`). It resets `@everyone`'s Send Messages
permission to neutral across the same set of channels, skipping any it cannot
change — and that includes any channel you had deliberately locked before the
raid, which comes back unlocked. Its reply tells you how many channels it
unlocked. Two things to know before you rely on it: the command refuses with
*Not Locked* unless the bot itself is holding a lockdown it started, and that
memory does not survive a bot restart, so a restart during a lockdown leaves
the channels locked with the command declining to lift them. The matching
`/lockdown activate` command, with the same **Manage Server**
(`ManageGuild`) requirement and an optional **reason**, locks the server by
hand, replies with the number of channels it locked, and refuses with
*Already Locked* while a lockdown is running. Both subcommands also need the
bot itself to hold **Manage Channels**, and both are the only place a lockdown
channel count is ever shown to you.

### Events tab

*Timeline of detected raids, account age blocks, and lockdown events.*

| Control | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Event rows | Newest first, 20 to a page. Each row carries a type badge — **Join Spike**, **Account Age**, **Nuke Attempt**, or **Lockdown** — the reason where one was recorded, the action taken, a *Users* count, the account age in days for an account-age block, and the date and time. The *Users* count reads **1** on every Join Spike and Account Age row, because each of those rows covers the single member it was raised for; Nuke Attempt and Lockdown rows carry no *Users* count at all. |
| **Previous** / **Next** | Move through the history 20 at a time. They appear only once more than 20 events exist. |

Empty reads *No raid events recorded yet. Events will appear here when raid
detection triggers.* — which, as noted in the Prerequisites, is also what an
admin lacking View Events sees.

Two things the timeline does not tell you. A *Nuke Attempt* row never names
the person who was quarantined, so pair it with your server's audit log to
find out who it was. And a row proves the detector fired, not that the
punishment landed: when the bot lacked the authority to kick, ban, or time
the member out, the event is recorded exactly the same way.

Lockdown rows come from three places and read slightly differently: an
automatic lockdown from a join spike says *Automatic raid lockdown triggered
by join spike*, a hand-run `/lockdown activate` carries the reason its runner
typed (or *Manual lockdown* when they typed none), and a `/lockdown lift`
row carries no reason at all — only its action. Lockdown rows from the
commands are recorded whether or not anti-raid is switched on.

## Related features

**Moderation**, **Logs**, and **Permissions** each have their own entry in
the guild sidebar and their own page in the dashboard, and each has a page in
this guide — [Moderation](/guide/features/moderation),
[Logging](/guide/features/logging), and
[Permissions](/guide/features/permissions). Here is where each meets
anti-raid.

- **Moderation** — the place a punishment you hand out yourself is recorded.
  Anti-raid actions are kept apart from it: a member kicked, banned, or timed
  out by a raid detector gets a raid event, not a moderation case, and does
  not appear in that member's case history.
- **Logs** — the member join log, which never records a join that anti-raid
  actioned, and the channel and role deletion logs, which do record the
  deletions behind a nuke attempt.
- **Permissions** — where the View Config, Manage Config, and View Events
  permissions above are granted to dashboard roles and to individual admins.
