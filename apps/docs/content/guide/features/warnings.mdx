Warnings give your moderators three slash commands for issuing, reviewing,
and clearing warnings, a dashboard page listing every warning in the server,
and a set of thresholds that make the bot time out, kick, or ban a member
automatically the moment their warning count reaches one.

## Prerequisites

- **The bot is in your server.** The dashboard refuses every warnings
  request for a server the bot has not joined.
- **Nothing has to be switched on first.** A server that has never opened
  the Warnings page behaves as though DM on Warn is on, Require Reason is
  off, and Max Warnings is 0. `/warn` works as soon as the bot is in the
  server; there is no enable switch for warnings.
- **Your moderators hold Manage Messages.** All three commands — `/warn`,
  `/warnings`, and `/clearwarnings` — are gated on **Manage Messages**, and
  each also re-checks that permission when it runs. `/warn` additionally
  needs the *bot* to hold Manage Messages, and refuses to run when it does
  not.
- **You can open the dashboard for the server.** Access goes to the server
  owner and to members whose Discord permissions include **Administrator**
  or **Manage Server**. Anyone else is turned away with a permission error.
- **If the permission system is switched on** for your server — on the
  **Permissions** page in the sidebar — the Warnings page splits across
  three permissions in the **Moderation** group: **View Warnings** to read
  the warning list, **Manage Warnings** to delete warnings or clear a
  member's whole history, and **Manage Punishments** for both the Escalation
  and the Settings tabs, reading as well as changing. The **Warnings** entry
  in the guild sidebar is shown only to admins holding View Warnings. The
  silence is on the reading side only: a tab you are not allowed to read
  comes up empty, with nothing on screen saying you lack the permission,
  while an action you go ahead and attempt does report the refusal in a
  message. Without
  Manage Punishments, the Settings tab shows its *Warning Settings* heading
  with no controls under it, so read that empty tab as "not shown to me,"
  never as "nothing is configured." The Escalation tab behaves differently:
  it reads *No escalation rules configured.* — again regardless of how many
  rules the server actually has — and below that it still draws the full
  **Add Escalation Rule** form. Threshold, Action, the Duration field, and
  **Add Rule** all appear and accept clicks, but pressing Add Rule is
  refused by the server and raises *Insufficient permissions*; no rule is
  created.

## Using the warning commands

### Issuing a warning

`/warn` takes a required **user** option and an optional **reason** of up to
500 characters. Its permission gate is **Manage Messages**. Leaving the
reason out records the warning with the text *No reason provided* — unless
Require Reason is on, in which case the command refuses with *A reason is
required to warn this member.* and nothing is recorded.

The command refuses three targets outright: yourself (*You cannot warn
yourself.*), the bot (*You cannot warn the bot.*), and any member whose
highest role is equal to or above yours (*You cannot warn a member with an
equal or higher role.*).

On success the bot replies in the channel with the member's name, the
reason, the member's new total as **Warning #N**, and the warning's ID. If
DM on Warn is on, the bot then tries to send the member a direct message
naming your server, the reason, and their total warning count. The attempt
comes after any escalation punishment has already been carried out, so a
warning that trips a kick or ban threshold reaches this point with the
member already removed from your server. A member with DMs closed is warned
anyway, and either way nothing about a DM that does not go through appears
in the reply.

### Reviewing warnings

`/warnings` takes a required **user** option and posts that member's warning
history, ten per page, newest first. Its permission gate is **Manage
Messages**. Each line carries the warning's ID, the moderator who issued it,
the reason, and how long ago it was issued; the footer counts the pages and
the total. When there is more than one page, **Previous** and **Next**
buttons appear — only the moderator who ran the command can press them, and
they stop working and disappear from the message two minutes after it was
posted. Running the command again gives you a fresh set.

### Clearing warnings

`/clearwarnings` takes a required **user** option and an optional **id**.
Its permission gate is **Manage Messages**.

With an **id**, it removes that single warning and confirms with *Warning
#N has been removed from* the member's display name. If that ID does not exist in this server, or belongs
to a different member than the one you named, nothing is deleted and the
bot replies *Warning #N was not found for this user.*

Without an **id**, it deletes every warning that member has and reports how
many were cleared. A member with no warnings gets *has no warnings to
clear.* instead.

Deleting warnings lowers the member's total, so a threshold they had already
passed can be reached — and fire — a second time as they are warned again.

### What escalation does

Every time `/warn` succeeds, the bot recounts that member's warnings in this
server and compares the new total against your escalation thresholds. When
the total is **exactly equal** to a threshold, the bot carries out that
threshold's action — timeout, kick, or ban — with the reason *Warn
escalation: N warnings*, and appends a line to the `/warn` reply naming the
action and the threshold. A total that overshoots a threshold does not
trigger it, which is why deleting warnings can bring a member back down to a
threshold that then fires again.

A timeout rule with no duration set times the member out for one hour.

If the action fails — most often because the member outranks the bot, or the
bot lacks the permission for that action — the warning is still recorded,
but the escalation line does not appear in the reply and nothing else is
posted in Discord.

## What each dashboard control does

The **Warnings** page is in the guild sidebar, below Moderation. It opens
with three counters — **Total Warnings**, **Escalation Rules** (how many
thresholds you have configured), and **DM on Warn** (Enabled or Disabled) —
above three tabs.

**Total Warnings** follows the Warning Log tab's user filter rather than
staying fixed on the server: with the filter box empty it counts every
warning in the server, and with a user ID in the box it counts only that
member's warnings.

### Warning Log tab

- **Filter by User ID...** — pasting a Discord user ID here narrows the table
  to that member's warnings. It matches the ID exactly; a username does not
  work.
- **Clear All** — this button exists only while the filter box has something
  in it; with the box empty there is no button next to it at all. Pressing it
  deletes every warning belonging to the user ID in the box, then empties the
  box.
- **The table** lists each warning's ID, the warned user's ID, the
  moderator's ID, the reason, and the date, newest first, ten rows to a
  page, with **Previous** and **Next** below when there is more than one
  page.
- **The trash icon** on a row deletes that one warning. It asks *Are you
  sure you want to delete this item? This action cannot be undone.* first,
  and there is no undo afterwards.

### Escalation tab

The table under **Punishment Escalation** lists your existing rules by
**Threshold**, **Action**, and **Duration** — the duration column shows the
timeout length rounded down to whole minutes, and a dash for kick and ban
rules, which take no duration. A timeout rule saved with the duration field
left empty also shows a dash here, even though it times the member out for
one hour.

The **Add Escalation Rule** form below it creates one:

- **Threshold** — the exact warning count that fires this rule. It must be 1
  or higher; anything else is rejected with *Threshold must be a positive
  number*. Each threshold can hold only one rule, so adding a second rule at
  a number already in the table fails and nothing is added — remove the
  existing rule first.
- **Action** — **Timeout**, **Kick**, or **Ban**. This is what the bot does
  to the member when their warning count hits the threshold.
- **Duration (seconds)** — shown only when Action is Timeout. This is how
  long the timeout lasts; leaving it empty makes the timeout one hour.
- **Add Rule** — saves the rule. It takes effect on the next `/warn`; the
  bot does not go back and apply a new rule to members who are already past
  its threshold.
- **The trash icon** on a rule removes it, after asking *Are you sure you
  want to remove this escalation rule? This action cannot be undone.*
  Members already timed out, kicked, or banned by that rule are not
  affected.

### Settings tab

- **DM on Warn** — on by default. While it is on, the bot tries to send each
  member you warn a direct message naming your server, the reason, and their
  total warning count. It is an attempt, not a guarantee: the bot carries out
  any escalation punishment first and sends the DM second, so a warning that
  trips a kick or ban threshold has already removed the member from your
  server by the time the message is attempted, and a member with DMs closed
  never receives one either. A DM that does not go through is passed over in
  silence — nothing about it appears in the `/warn` reply. Switching the
  setting off means warnings are recorded and announced in the channel only,
  and the member is never messaged.
- **Require Reason** — off by default. Switching it on makes `/warn` refuse
  any warning issued without a reason instead of recording it as *No reason
  provided*.
- **Max Warnings** — 0 by default, and the field accepts 0 or any positive
  number. The number you type is saved, and the field shows it again when
  you reopen the tab — but nothing in the bot acts on it: it does not cap
  how many warnings a member can receive, and it does not punish anyone on
  its own. Use an Escalation rule
  when you want a warning count to have a consequence.

Every control on this tab saves the moment you change it — there is no Save
button. A change the server refuses shows the message the server sent back:
a refusal because you lack Manage Punishments reads *Insufficient
permissions*. The generic *Failed to update setting* is the fallback for
every other kind of failure — any one where no readable message came back,
whether because the request never reached the server or because what came
back was not something the dashboard could read.

## Related features

- **Moderation** — bans, kicks, timeouts, and the numbered case history.
  Warnings are stored separately from cases: `/warn` records a warning and
  does not open a moderation case, which is why the Moderation page's case
  filter has nothing to show under *Warn*.
- **Permissions** — creating dashboard roles and granting View Warnings,
  Manage Warnings, and Manage Punishments, which is what the Prerequisites
  section above refers to.
- **Logs** — the channels the bot posts server event messages into, if you
  want a written record of activity beyond the warning list itself.
